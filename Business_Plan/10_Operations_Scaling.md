## 3. Process Scaling

### 3.1 Event Operations — Standard Operating Procedures

**Pre-event (2 weeks before)**:
1. Venue confirmed and deposit paid
2. Theme, dress code, and rules finalized
3. Ticket sales opened (Tuti + own channels)
4. DM team assigned and briefed
5. Supplies checklist completed (tokens, wristbands, first aid, consent forms)
6. Marketing push (social media, Telegram, WhatsApp)
7. Photography/DJ/performer contracts signed
8. Emergency contacts and protocols reviewed

**Event day**:
1. Setup crew arrives 3-4 hours early
2. Venue walkthrough: safety check, emergency exits, first aid station
3. Sound/lighting check
4. DM pre-event briefing
5. Door operations: check-in, vetting verification, wristband assignment, token distribution
6. Event runs per schedule
7. Last call and wind-down protocol
8. Tear-down and venue cleanup
9. Safety debrief with DM team

**Post-event (within 72 hours)**:
1. Feedback survey sent to attendees
2. DM incident reports collected
3. Financial reconciliation (revenue vs. costs)
4. Social media content published (approved photos)
5. Post-mortem meeting with team
6. Lessons documented in `Investigacion/02_Organization_Toolkit/Administracion/08_postmortem_eventos.md`
7. Token balances updated in system

### 3.2 Fun4Me Order Fulfillment SOP

1. Order received (website, WhatsApp, or event)
2. Payment confirmed
3. Order picked and packed (discreet packaging protocol)
4. Shipping label generated
5. Courier dispatched (moto delivery for Asuncion, encomienda for interior)
6. Tracking info sent to customer via WhatsApp
7. Delivery confirmation
8. 7-day follow-up satisfaction message
9. Review request sent (14 days post-delivery)

### 3.3 Membership Vetting SOP

1. Application received (Google Form / Telegram bot)
2. Application reviewed within 48 hours
3. Background check (social media review per OSINT protocol)
4. Interview scheduled (voice/video call)
5. Interview conducted by trained ambassador
6. Decision communicated within 72 hours
7. If approved: welcome sequence initiated, buddy assigned, probation period starts
8. If declined: respectful communication with general reason (no specifics)
9. Probation period (2 events): community feedback collected
10. Full membership confirmed or probation extended

## 4. Financial Scaling

### 4.1 Cash Management Rules

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Reserve fund** | Maintain 3 months of operating costs in reserve at all times |
| **Event float** | Pre-fund each event budget separately — never use reserves for event costs |
| **Inventory budget** | Never exceed 2 months of projected sales in inventory |
| **Marketing cap** | Never exceed 10% of revenue on marketing (excluding content creation) |
| **Salary cap** | Team costs should not exceed 50% of revenue |
| **Reinvestment rate** | Reinvest 70% of profits in Year 1-2, 50% in Year 3+ |

### 4.2 Revenue Milestones

| Monthly Revenue | What It Means | What to Do |
|---|---|---|
| **Gs. 10,000,000** | Proof of concept | Invest in marketing and next event |
| **Gs. 25,000,000** | Traction | Hire first part-time team member |
| **Gs. 50,000,000** | Growth phase | Hire operations manager, expand event calendar |
| **Gs. 80,000,000** | Approaching break-even | Invest in technology (app, advanced tools) |
| **Gs. 100,000,000** | Profitable | Consider permanent space, geographic expansion |
| **Gs. 150,000,000** | Scale phase | Full team, multi-city operations |
| **Gs. 250,000,000+** | Institution | Advisory board, investor considerations, regional expansion |

### 4.3 Investment Readiness

If seeking external investment, prepare:
- Audited financials (even informal — clean books matter)
- Customer and community metrics dashboard
- Growth trajectory with monthly data points
- Clear use of funds plan
- Exit or return-on-investment path for investor
- Legal structure supporting investment (SAS allows this)

## 6. Community Scaling

### 6.1 Community Health Indicators

Monitor these to ensure community quality scales with quantity:

| Indicator | Healthy | Warning | Critical |
|---|---|---|---|
| **New member application rate** | Steady growth | Sudden spike (viral but unvetted) | Decline (awareness problem) |
| **Vetting completion rate** | >70% | 50-70% | <50% |
| **Event attendance rate** (tickets sold vs. showed up) | >80% | 60-80% | <60% |
| **Member retention** (renewing month-over-month) | >75% | 60-75% | <60% |
| **NPS (Net Promoter Score)** | >50 | 30-50 | <30 |
| **Safety incidents** | 0 serious | Any minor | Any serious |
| **Community drama frequency** | Rare, resolved quickly | Monthly conflicts | Unresolved ongoing conflicts |
| **Volunteer availability** | More volunteers than needed | Just enough | Struggle to staff events |
| **Online engagement** | Active daily discussion | Sporadic engagement | Silent groups |

### 6.2 Dunbar's Number and Community Structure

Communities face natural scaling challenges at key thresholds:

| Community Size | Challenge | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| **15** (intimate group) | Everyone knows everyone — high trust, easy to manage | Enjoy it while it lasts |
| **50** (tribe) | Start losing personal connections, cliques form | Create sub-groups by interest, assign ambassadors |
| **150** (Dunbar's number) | Impossible to know everyone personally | Formalize governance, create roles, document processes |
| **500+** | Community-within-community dynamics | Chapters/interest groups, tiered events, regional structure |
| **1,000+** | Institution, not community | Professional management, elected/appointed leadership |

### 6.3 Scaling Community Culture

As community grows, culture must be actively maintained:

1. **Document core values** and reference them in all communications
2. **Onboard intentionally** — every new member should understand the culture before participating
3. **Recognize and reward culture carriers** — people who embody community values
4. **Address violations quickly** — one unaddressed incident teaches 100 people the wrong lesson
5. **Create traditions** — recurring rituals, inside references, community stories
6. **Elevate community voices** — let members lead, teach, create
7. **Governance transparency** — as the community grows, decisions affect more people; be transparent about how decisions are made

## 7. Quality Standards

### 7.1 Event Quality Checklist

Every event must meet these minimum standards:

- [ ] Venue is clean, safe, and appropriate for event type
- [ ] At least 2 trained DMs present per 50 attendees
- [ ] First aid kit on site
- [ ] Emergency exits clearly marked and unobstructed
- [ ] Sound system works and is at appropriate volume
- [ ] Consent rules posted visibly
- [ ] Check-in process is smooth (< 5 minutes per person)
- [ ] Tokens/wristbands distributed correctly
- [ ] Theme is executed (decor, music, lighting match the theme)
- [ ] Photographer has consent forms ready
- [ ] Post-event feedback mechanism in place
- [ ] Post-event debrief scheduled

### 7.2 Fun4Me Quality Standards

- All products inspected before listing (body-safe materials only)
- Product descriptions include safety information and usage guidance
- All packaging is discreet and secure
- Orders shipped within 24 hours of payment confirmation
- Customer inquiries responded to within 2 hours (WhatsApp) / 4 hours (DM)
- Returns processed within 7 business days
- Product reviews are genuine (verified purchases only)

### 7.3 Content Quality Standards

- All public content reviewed for brand alignment before publishing
- No attendee faces in public content without written consent
- Educational content reviewed by qualified professional (ECIS partnership)
- All content in Spanish (with English option where feasible)
- Consistent visual branding across all channels
- No content that could be construed as promoting illegal activity
- Algorithm-safe versions of content for mainstream social media

## 8. Crisis Management Scaling

### 8.1 Crisis Types and Response Levels

| Level | Crisis Type | Response Team | Timeline |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Minor** | Small complaint, social media negativity, minor logistics issue | Social media manager + relevant lead | 24 hours |
| **2 — Moderate** | Media inquiry, venue cancellation, community conflict, minor safety concern | Founders + Safety Director + Legal | 4-12 hours |
| **3 — Serious** | Safety incident at event, major media attack, regulatory inquiry | Full leadership + Legal + PR spokesperson | Immediate |
| **4 — Critical** | Serious injury, criminal allegation, organized opposition campaign, government action | Full leadership + Legal + Crisis PR + Emergency team | Immediate, 24/7 |

### 8.2 Crisis Communication Principles

1. **Speed**: Acknowledge the situation quickly (within hours, not days)
2. **Transparency**: Share what you know, what you don't, and what you're doing
3. **Empathy**: Lead with concern for affected parties
4. **Accountability**: Own mistakes, don't deflect
5. **Action**: Announce concrete steps being taken
6. **Follow-up**: Update stakeholders as situation evolves
7. **Documentation**: Record everything for legal and learning purposes

### 8.3 Pre-Prepared Crisis Statements

Have template statements ready for:
- Safety incident at event
- Media misrepresentation / "orgy" framing
- Conservative protest or public opposition
- Social media account suspension
- Venue cancellation
- Team member misconduct
- Data breach
- Regulatory inquiry
