## 1. Risk Register

### 1.1 Critical Risks (Existential Threats)

| # | Risk | Probability | Impact | Mitigation | Response Plan |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | **Serious safety incident at event** (assault, injury, medical emergency) | Low | Critical | DM training, safety protocols, first aid, vetting, emergency contacts, incident response plan | Immediate medical response → isolate situation → document → support affected parties → notify authorities if required → internal investigation → transparent community update → implement improvements |
| C2 | **Criminal allegation against organizer or community leader** | Very Low | Critical | Background checks on leadership, clear codes of conduct, insurance | Legal counsel immediately → cooperate with authorities → suspend accused from roles → independent investigation → transparent communication |
| C3 | **Regulatory crackdown** (new laws restricting adult events/commerce) | Low | Critical | Political relationships, legal monitoring, advocacy partnerships, contingency plans | Legal review → assess compliance requirements → pivot if necessary → advocacy response → community communication |
| C4 | **Major reputation crisis** (negative media storm) | Medium | Critical | Crisis communication plan, media training, institutional partnerships that provide cover | Activate crisis communication → spokesperson responds within 4 hours → provide facts and context → rally institutional allies → document actual impact |

### 1.2 High Risks (Significant Disruption)

| # | Risk | Probability | Impact | Mitigation | Response |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | **Social media deplatforming** (account suspension/ban) | High | High | Diversified channels, email list, Telegram, content backups, algorithm-safe content | Migrate to backup accounts → notify community via email/Telegram → appeal ban → adjust content strategy → build owned channels |
| H2 | **Venue cancellation** (last-minute) | Medium | High | Multiple venue relationships, backup venues identified, contracts with cancellation terms | Activate backup venue → notify attendees → negotiate with original venue → document and learn |
| H3 | **Conservative organized opposition** (boycotts, protests, doxxing) | Medium | High | Wellness positioning, institutional allies, legal preparedness, digital security | Do not engage publicly → document threats → legal review → security assessment → rally allies → continue operations |
| H4 | **Key person burnout or departure** | High | High | Cross-training, documentation, distributed leadership, this knowledge base | Redistribute responsibilities → recruit replacement → knowledge transfer → community continuity |
| H5 | **Financial crisis** (cash flow emergency) | Medium | High | 3-month reserve fund, conservative budgeting, diversified revenue | Cut non-essential costs → emergency fundraising → community communication → pivot to lower-cost programming |
| H6 | **Data breach** (member data exposed) | Low | High | Encryption, access controls, minimal data collection, privacy policies | Contain breach → assess scope → notify affected members within 72 hours → law enforcement if criminal → improve security → transparency |

### 1.3 Medium Risks (Manageable Disruption)

| # | Risk | Probability | Impact | Mitigation | Response |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | **Community drama** (interpersonal conflicts going public) | High | Medium | Clear governance, conflict resolution process, moderation, community culture | Private mediation → enforce community guidelines → remove bad actors if needed → address community impact |
| M2 | **Competitor with more capital** | Medium | Medium | Community moat, brand loyalty, first-mover advantage, superior events | Focus on community value, don't engage in price wars → differentiate on quality and trust |
| M3 | **Economic downturn** (reduced consumer spending) | Medium | Medium | Flexible pricing, free/low-cost event options, essential products focus | Adjust pricing → add more free events → focus on community value over premium experiences |
| M4 | **Supplier/product quality issue** (defective products) | Low | Medium | Body-safe materials only, trusted suppliers, product testing, return policy | Remove product → notify customers → offer refund/exchange → investigate supplier → strengthen QA |
| M5 | **Event underperformance** (low attendance) | Medium | Medium | Pre-sales, marketing calendar, diverse event types, community feedback | Post-mortem analysis → adjust format/pricing/promotion → consider merging or canceling underperforming formats |
| M6 | **Insurance gap** | High | Medium | Research available event insurance in Paraguay, consult with insurance broker | Identify and secure appropriate coverage as soon as available |

### 1.4 Low Risks (Minor Disruption)

| # | Risk | Probability | Impact | Mitigation | Response |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | **Content theft/plagiarism** | Medium | Low | Watermarks, brand mentions, terms of use | DMCA takedown → document → consider as compliment if minor |
| L2 | **Bad online reviews** | Medium | Low | Excellent service, respond to complaints quickly, request reviews from satisfied customers | Respond professionally → address legitimate concerns → don't engage with trolls |
| L3 | **Equipment failure at event** (sound, lighting) | Low | Low | Equipment checks before events, backup equipment, technical crew | Improvise → use backup → refund if event is significantly impacted |
| L4 | **Weather impact on outdoor events** | Medium | Low | Indoor backup plan, weather monitoring, flexible scheduling | Move to indoor backup → reschedule if necessary → communicate proactively |

## 2. Safety & Security Framework

### 2.1 Physical Security — Events

| Measure | Standard Events | Large Events (Sexpo) |
|---|---|---|
| **Door security** | 1-2 trained staff | Professional security team |
| **ID verification** | Check government ID for age (18+) | Check government ID + cross-reference guest list |
| **DMs on site** | 2 per 50 attendees | 3-4 per 50 attendees |
| **First aid** | First aid kit + trained person | First aid station + trained responder |
| **Emergency exits** | 2 minimum, clearly marked | All exits identified, emergency lighting |
| **Emergency contacts** | List posted backstage | List posted + security briefed |
| **Medical emergencies** | Call SAME (131) or nearest hospital | On-site medical support + ambulance on standby (large events) |
| **Fire safety** | Fire extinguisher, exit routes | Full fire safety plan, coordination with bomberos |

### 2.2 Consent & Safety Protocols

| Protocol | Description |
|---|---|
| **Consent framework** | RACK (Risk-Aware Consensual Kink) as community standard |
| **Safe words** | "Rojo" (stop), "Amarillo" (slow down), universal for all events |
| **Token/wristband system** | Visual consent indicators at Maskarada events |
| **No-go list** | Confidential list of banned individuals (managed by Safety Director) |
| **Photography rules** | No photos without explicit consent, designated photo areas only |
| **Substance policy** | Clear policy on alcohol consumption, zero tolerance for illegal substances |
| **DM intervention protocol** | Check-in, verbal warning, scene stop, removal — escalating response |
| **Incident reporting** | Anonymous reporting available, mandatory reporting by DMs for any concern |

### 2.3 Digital Security

| Measure | Implementation |
|---|---|
| **Account security** | 2FA on all organizational accounts (social media, email, banking) |
| **Password management** | Use password manager (Bitwarden/1Password), unique passwords for every service |
| **Data encryption** | Encrypt sensitive member data at rest (AES-256) |
| **Access control** | Role-based access — not everyone needs access to everything |
| **Communication encryption** | Use Signal or encrypted Telegram for sensitive internal communication |
| **Backup strategy** | Daily automated backups of website, databases, documents |
| **Device security** | Full-disk encryption on staff devices, screen lock, remote wipe capability |
| **Social engineering awareness** | Train staff on phishing, pretexting, and social engineering attacks |
| **Member privacy** | Never share member information between members without explicit consent |
| **Content security** | Pre-approve all public content, watermark photos, control distribution |

## 4. Community Risk Management

### 4.1 Predator Prevention

| Measure | Description |
|---|---|
| **Vetting process** | Multi-step application, social media review, interview before membership |
| **Probation period** | New members on probation for first 2 events — community feedback collected |
| **Reference system** | Ask for references from existing community members or other communities |
| **OSINT screening** | Responsible social media review per protocols in Seguridad/03_vetting_osint_avanzado.md |
| **Red flag database** | Internal, confidential record of banned individuals and reasons |
| **Cross-community communication** | Share ban information with trusted partner communities (with legal guidance) |
| **Training** | DMs and ambassadors trained to identify concerning behavior patterns |
| **Reporting culture** | Actively encourage reporting — "if something feels off, tell a DM" |

### 4.2 Conflict Resolution Process

| Level | Situation | Response | Decision Maker |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Minor** | Personality conflict, minor disagreement | Private conversation, mediation offer | Ambassador or Community Manager |
| **2 — Moderate** | Repeated boundary issues, complaints from multiple members | Formal mediation, written warning | Safety Director + Community Manager |
| **3 — Serious** | Consent violation, harassment allegation | Investigation, suspension during process, potential ban | Safety Director + Founders |
| **4 — Critical** | Assault, criminal behavior, imminent threat | Immediate ban, contact authorities, support victim | Founders + Legal Counsel |

### 4.3 Community Culture Protection

As community grows, actively protect against:

| Threat | Signs | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| **Cliquishness** | Newcomers feel excluded, same small group dominates | Newcomer buddy system, rotating event formats, leadership diversity |
| **Drama culture** | Public conflicts, gossip, social media callouts | Clear moderation, private conflict resolution, drama = removal policy |
| **Entitlement** | Members expecting special treatment, ignoring rules | Consistent rule enforcement regardless of status or tenure |
| **Predatory behavior** | Repeated boundary pushing, targeting newcomers | Vetting, probation, DM training, reporting culture |
| **Burnout** | Volunteers and leaders overworking, losing enthusiasm | Appreciation, rotation, boundaries, compensation when possible |
| **Homogeneity** | Community lacks diversity (age, gender, orientation, race, income) | Intentional inclusion programming, diverse event types, sliding scale pricing |

## 5. Financial Risk Management

### 5.1 Financial Controls

| Control | Description |
|---|---|
| **Separation of duties** | No single person controls all financial processes |
| **Dual authorization** | Large expenditures (>Gs. 5,000,000) require two approvals |
| **Monthly reconciliation** | Bank statements reconciled with books monthly |
| **Receipt documentation** | All expenditures documented with receipts |
| **Event budgets** | Every event has pre-approved budget, actual vs. budget tracked |
| **Tax compliance** | Monthly SET filings, annual statements, IVA compliance |
| **Cash handling** | Count cash with witness, deposit within 24 hours, document all cash transactions |
| **Inventory controls** | Regular inventory counts, reconcile with sales records |

### 5.2 Financial Contingency Plans

| Scenario | Trigger | Response |
|---|---|---|
| **Cash flow crunch** | Reserve falls below 1 month of operating costs | Cut discretionary spending → postpone hires → reduce event frequency → focus on highest-ROI activities |
| **Event financial loss** | Event costs exceed revenue by >30% | Post-mortem analysis → adjust pricing or format → cancel future events in same format until profitable model proven |
| **Inventory write-off** | >20% of inventory unsold after 6 months | Liquidation sale → donate unsaleable items → adjust purchasing to match demand data |
| **Economic recession** | GDP contraction or guarani devaluation >20% | Reduce pricing → increase free programming → focus on essentials → community-funded model |
| **Sudden large expense** | Legal action, equipment failure, emergency | Use reserve fund → emergency fundraise → insurance claim if applicable |

## 7. Operational Risk Management

### 7.1 Business Continuity

| Scenario | Continuity Plan |
|---|---|
| **Physical space unavailable** (venue, office, storage) | Cloud-based operations, backup venue list, equipment stored at multiple locations |
| **Key team member unavailable** | Cross-trained team, documented processes, shared access to systems |
| **Internet/power outage** | Mobile hotspot backup, battery backup for critical devices, pre-downloaded resources |
| **Pandemic or health crisis** | Pivot to online programming, postpone physical events, maintain digital community |
| **Natural disaster** | Postpone events, community check-in, disaster relief mode, resume when safe |
| **Supply chain disruption** | Maintain 2-3 month inventory of bestsellers, multiple supplier relationships, local sourcing options |

### 7.2 Vendor Risk Management

| Vendor Type | Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Venue owners** | Last-minute cancellation, price increase, conservative pressure | Written contracts, backup venues, relationship management |
| **Product suppliers** | Quality issues, delivery delays, price increases | Multiple suppliers, quality testing, buffer stock |
| **Technology providers** | Service outage, price change, platform closure | Data portability, backup platforms, open-source alternatives |
| **Payment processors** | Account closure (adult content policies), delays | Multiple processors, cash backup, crypto option |
| **Freelancers/contractors** | Unavailability, quality issues | Backup freelancers, clear contracts, relationships with multiple providers |

## 8. Risk Review & Governance

### 8.1 Risk Review Cadence

| Review | Frequency | Participants | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Post-event safety debrief** | After every event | DM team + Safety Director | Incidents, near-misses, improvements |
| **Monthly risk check** | Monthly | Founders + Safety Director | Review risk register, update mitigation status |
| **Quarterly risk assessment** | Quarterly | Full leadership team | Comprehensive review, update risk register, scenario planning |
| **Annual risk audit** | Annually | Full team + Legal Counsel | Full risk register review, insurance review, legal compliance check, update all response plans |

### 8.2 Incident Documentation

Every incident (no matter how minor) should be documented:

1. **Date, time, location**
2. **People involved** (names, roles, membership status)
3. **What happened** (factual, detailed, chronological)
4. **Who responded** (DM names, actions taken)
5. **Outcome** (resolution, follow-up needed)
6. **Evidence** (photos, messages, witness statements — with consent)
7. **Root cause analysis** (what could have prevented this?)
8. **Recommendations** (changes to prevent recurrence)
9. **Follow-up status** (were recommendations implemented?)

Store all incident documentation in encrypted, restricted-access storage. Only Safety Director and Founders have access.

### 8.3 Learning from Incidents

After every significant incident:
1. Conduct root cause analysis within 72 hours
2. Identify systemic failures (not just individual blame)
3. Propose specific, actionable improvements
4. Implement improvements before next event
5. Communicate changes to team (without breaching confidentiality)
6. Review whether existing protocols were followed
7. Update protocols if gaps identified
