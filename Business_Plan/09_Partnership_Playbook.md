# Partnership Playbook — Complete Guide to Every Alliance

## Building the Network That Makes the Ecosystem Unstoppable

This document provides a comprehensive strategy for every type of partnership the ecosystem can pursue — from health institutions to alcohol brands, from international communities to local artisans.

---

## 1. Partnership Philosophy

### 1.1 Core Principles

Every partnership must pass three tests:

1. **Mutual value**: Both parties get something concrete — no one-sided relationships
2. **Brand alignment**: Partner must not compromise our safety, consent, or inclusion values
3. **Community benefit**: Partnership must ultimately serve our community, not just our revenue

### 1.2 Partnership Tiers

| Tier | Commitment | Duration | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Strategic** | Deep integration, shared resources, long-term | 1+ years | ECIS, UNES, SOMOSGAY |
| **Commercial** | Revenue exchange, co-marketing | 6-12 months | Hotels, venues, brands |
| **Collaborative** | Event-by-event or project-based | Per project | Performers, educators, photographers |
| **Referral** | Simple cross-promotion | Ongoing | Therapists, health professionals |
| **Community** | Peer relationship with other kink/wellness orgs | Ongoing | BA kink scene, OpenKink |

## 3. Venue Partnerships — The Foundation

### 3.1 Venue Partnership Structure

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Minimum viable venues** | 3-4 reliable venues for different event types |
| **Contract type** | Recurring rental agreement with preferential rates for multi-event bookings |
| **Key negotiation points** | Minimum spend vs. fixed rental, bar revenue share, setup/teardown time, noise/hour limits |
| **Red flags** | Venues that impose content restrictions, unreliable management, history of last-minute cancellations |

### 3.2 Venue Pipeline

| Venue Type | For What Events | Examples | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nightclub** (200-400 capacity) | Maskarada Noche, Sexpo after-party | Tango Asuncion, Palo Santo | Critical |
| **Bar/restaurant** (50-100 capacity) | Munches, Socials, small workshops | Rock bars, LGBTQ+ friendly venues | Critical |
| **Private salon** (20-40 capacity) | Maskarada Intima, VIP, small workshops | Speakeasies, private event rooms | High |
| **Quinta/estate** (50-200 capacity) | Retreats, summer events, outdoor play | Aregua quintas, San Ber properties | Medium |
| **Hotel event space** (200-500 capacity) | Sexpo, conferences, large workshops | Sheraton, Dazzler, boutique hotels | Medium |
| **Convention center** (500-1000+) | Future large-scale Sexpo | Centro de Convenciones | Year 3+ |
| **Art gallery/cultural space** | Exhibitions, film screenings, poetry | Independent galleries | Medium |
| **Co-working/meeting room** | Team meetings, small workshops, vetting interviews | WeWork, local coworking | Low cost |

### 3.3 Venue Relationship Management

**Monthly cadence**:
- Post-event thank you + debrief with venue manager
- Share positive feedback, attendance numbers, bar revenue generated
- Discuss improvements and next event logistics
- Book next 2-3 events in advance for preferential dates

**Backup plan**: Always have 2+ backup venues identified for every event type. If primary venue cancels (conservative pressure, double-booking, etc.), backup must be contactable within 24 hours.

## 8. Partnership Activation Playbook

### 8.1 How to Approach Any Partner

**Step 1: Research**
- Understand their mission, audience, and current partnerships
- Identify the mutual value proposition
- Find the right contact person

**Step 2: First Contact**
- Brief, professional email or message
- Lead with what you can offer THEM
- Include 1-page partnership proposal
- Reference any mutual connections

**Step 3: Meeting**
- Present the ecosystem, your audience, and your vision
- Listen to their needs and goals
- Propose specific, actionable first step (not the whole partnership)
- Follow up within 48 hours

**Step 4: Pilot**
- Start with a small, low-risk collaboration (one event, one product, one workshop)
- Over-deliver on the pilot
- Document results and share with partner

**Step 5: Formalize**
- Propose longer-term agreement based on pilot results
- Written agreement with clear terms (even informal partnerships benefit from documentation)
- Regular check-ins and performance reviews

### 8.2 Partnership Proposal Template

```
PARTNERSHIP PROPOSAL
Date: [date]
From: [Your Brand] — Paraguay Adult Wellness Ecosystem
To: [Partner Organization]

1. WHO WE ARE
   - Brief ecosystem overview (3 sentences)
   - Key metrics: community size, events per year, audience reach

2. WHY WE'RE REACHING OUT
   - Specific mutual opportunity identified
   - How their audience/products/services complement ours

3. WHAT WE PROPOSE
   - Specific partnership activity (be concrete)
   - Timeline
   - Resources each party contributes

4. WHAT THEY GET
   - Audience access (with numbers)
   - Brand exposure (channels, frequency)
   - Revenue opportunity (if applicable)

5. WHAT WE GET
   - Content/services/products/credibility
   - Revenue (if applicable)

6. NEXT STEP
   - Propose a meeting or call
   - Suggest a low-commitment pilot
```

### 8.3 Partnership Tracking

Maintain a partnership database with:
- Partner name and contact info
- Partnership tier and type
- Agreement terms and duration
- Key deliverables from each side
- Revenue generated (if applicable)
- Relationship health score (1-5)
- Next review date
- Notes and history

## 10. Partnership Risk Management

### 10.1 Red Flags

| Red Flag | Action |
|---|---|
| Partner demands editorial control over our content | Negotiate advisory role only, or walk away |
| Partner's values conflict with inclusion/consent | Do not proceed — reputation risk too high |
| Partner wants to be the only brand visible | Negotiate co-branding, not takeover |
| Partner has legal/regulatory issues | Due diligence before any association |
| Partner ghosts after initial enthusiasm | Set clear deadlines, move to backup partners |
| Partner wants free access without contribution | Require concrete value exchange |

### 10.2 Partnership Agreements

Even informal partnerships benefit from a written understanding covering:
- What each party will do (deliverables)
- Timeline and duration
- Financial terms (if any)
- Brand usage guidelines
- Confidentiality expectations
- Exit terms (how to end the partnership gracefully)
- Liability and insurance
- Data sharing and privacy obligations

### 10.3 Partnership Exit Protocol

If a partnership needs to end:
1. Communicate decision directly and respectfully
2. Honor all current commitments
3. Remove partner branding from future materials
4. Return any shared assets
5. Maintain positive relationship (community is small)
6. Document lessons learned
