## 1. Health & Wellness Programs

### 1.1 STI Testing Program

**What**: Free monthly rapid HIV/STI testing events at community venues and at every major event

**Why it matters**: Directly addresses public health need, provides institutional credibility, demonstrates community value beyond entertainment

**How to implement**:
1. Contact UNES and Ministry of Health HIV/AIDS department
2. Propose monthly testing events at community gatherings + presence at Sexpo
3. They provide: rapid test kits, trained counselors, educational materials
4. We provide: venue, audience, promotion, volunteer support
5. Document: number of tests administered, positive referrals, materials distributed
6. Report in annual impact report

**Partners**: UNES, Ministry of Health, Red Cross Paraguay
**Cost**: Gs. 0 (partner-funded) — our contribution is venue and audience
**Revenue**: None direct — value is credibility, community health, and sponsorship justification
**Timeline**: Can launch Month 1
**KPIs**: Tests per event, total annual tests, percentage of attendees tested

### 1.2 Sexual Health Consultations Referral Network

**What**: Curated network of kink-aware health professionals offering member discounts

**How to implement**:
1. Partner with ECIS to identify kink-aware therapists, sexologists, gynecologists
2. Create referral directory (digital, available to members)
3. Negotiate member discount rates (10-20% off standard consultation)
4. Track referrals and gather feedback to improve directory
5. Add professionals over time based on community needs

**Partners**: ECIS, private practitioners
**Cost**: Gs. 500,000 (directory creation and maintenance)
**Revenue**: Referral fees (optional) or pure community benefit
**Timeline**: Month 2-3
**KPIs**: Professionals in directory, referrals per month, member satisfaction

### 1.3 Harm Reduction Program

**What**: Free condoms, lubricant, harm reduction information at every event

**How to implement**:
1. Source free condom supply from Ministry of Health or sponsor brands (Prudence, Tulipan)
2. Create information cards: safe sex basics, consent, substance use
3. Set up discreet distribution table at every event
4. Train volunteers to answer basic health questions
5. Provide referral cards for professional services

**Cost**: Gs. 200,000/month (printing, supplies not covered by sponsors)
**Timeline**: Month 1
**KPIs**: Supplies distributed per event, referrals made

### 1.4 Pelvic Floor Health Program

**What**: Quarterly workshops on pelvic floor health for all genders

**How to implement**:
1. Partner with specialized physiotherapist
2. Schedule quarterly 90-minute workshops
3. Include anatomy education, exercise instruction, Q&A
4. Offer follow-up referral for individual assessment

**Partners**: Physiotherapy professionals
**Cost**: Gs. 500,000/workshop (instructor fee)
**Revenue**: Gs. 80,000-150,000/ticket x 15-25 attendees = Gs. 1.2-3.75M
**Timeline**: Quarter 2
**KPIs**: Attendance, participant satisfaction, professional referrals generated

## 2. Education Programs

### 2.1 BDSM 101 — Monthly Introductory Workshop

**What**: 2-hour comprehensive introduction to BDSM for absolute beginners

**Curriculum**:
1. What is BDSM? Debunking myths (20 min)
2. Consent frameworks: SSC, RACK, PRICK (20 min)
3. Common activities overview: bondage, D/s, impact, sensory, role play (30 min)
4. Safety basics: safe words, aftercare, communication (20 min)
5. Q&A and resource sharing (30 min)

**How to implement**:
1. Develop curriculum (use international resources + local cultural context)
2. Identify facilitator (community member with teaching ability or ECIS educator)
3. Book consistent venue (same place, same day of month)
4. Promote through all channels, position as entry-point event
5. Provide handout with resources, glossary, recommended reading

**Cost**: Gs. 500,000/workshop (venue + materials + facilitator)
**Revenue**: Gs. 50,000-100,000/ticket x 20-40 attendees = Gs. 1-4M
**Timeline**: Month 1-2
**KPIs**: Monthly attendance, post-workshop survey scores, conversion to membership

### 2.2 Shibari Progressive Track

**What**: Structured rope bondage curriculum from beginner to instructor certification

**Levels**:

| Level | Duration | Content | Price (Gs.) | Prerequisite |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Beginner** (Nudo 1) | 4 sessions x 2hr | Safety, basic ties, floorwork | 400,000 (series) | None |
| **Intermediate** (Nudo 2) | 6 sessions x 2hr | Complex ties, harnesses, body mechanics | 600,000 (series) | Nudo 1 complete |
| **Advanced** (Nudo 3) | 8 sessions x 3hr | Partial suspension, advanced patterns, aesthetics | 1,000,000 (series) | Nudo 2 complete |

**Revenue potential**: Gs. 2-5M/month with full pipeline

### 2.3 DM (Dungeon Monitor) Certification Program

**What**: 16-hour formal training and certification for event safety monitors

**Curriculum** (4 sessions x 4 hours):

| Session | Topics |
|---|---|
| **Session 1** | Role of a DM, community safety philosophy, observation skills, de-escalation |
| **Session 2** | Consent recognition, intervention techniques, verbal and non-verbal cues, safe words |
| **Session 3** | First aid basics (with Red Cross), medical emergencies at events, mental health crisis response |
| **Session 4** | Scenario training: role-play exercises simulating common incidents, incident report writing, legal considerations |

**How to implement**:
1. Develop curriculum (reference NLA-Austin DM Training Manual)
2. Partner with Red Cross for first aid component
3. Recruit experienced DMs from BA scene for initial training
4. Conduct training over 2 weekends or 4 consecutive weeks
5. Written test + practical assessment for certification
6. Recertification annually (4-hour refresher)

**Cost**: Gs. 2,000,000 setup (curriculum, materials, guest trainers)
**Revenue**: Gs. 500,000/participant x 8-12 participants = Gs. 4-6M per cohort
**Timeline**: Month 3-4 (before first play party)
**KPIs**: Certified DMs, incident-free events, participant satisfaction

### 2.4 Consent Educator Certification

**What**: 12-hour program training community members to lead consent workshops

**Curriculum** (3 sessions x 4 hours):

| Session | Topics |
|---|---|
| **Session 1** | Consent theory, frameworks (SSC/RACK/PRICK), cultural context, communication models |
| **Session 2** | Workshop facilitation skills, handling difficult questions, creating safe discussion spaces |
| **Session 3** | Practical: each participant facilitates a mini-workshop, receives feedback, graduates |

**Revenue**: Gs. 500,000/participant
**Output**: Certified consent educators who can lead free public workshops (community outreach)

## 3. Social Programs

### 3.1 Weekly Munch Program

**What**: The backbone of community building — regular casual meetups at vanilla venues

**How to implement**:
1. Select a consistent venue (bar or restaurant, kink-friendly but not kink-themed)
2. Choose a consistent day/time (e.g., every Tuesday at 7pm)
3. Designate a munch host (ambassador or volunteer)
4. Host announces on Telegram/FetLife, introduces new attendees
5. No kink activities, no dress code — just conversation and connection
6. Munch host ensures inclusivity, introduces shy newcomers, guides conversation

**Cost**: Gs. 0 (everyone pays for their own food/drink)
**Revenue**: None direct — this is community infrastructure
**Timeline**: Week 1
**KPIs**: Weekly attendance, new faces per munch, conversions to membership

**Variants**:
- **Coffee Kink** (morning cafe meetup, monthly)
- **Afterwork Kink** (Friday happy hour, bi-weekly)
- **Ladies' Munch** (women-only, monthly)
- **LGBTQ+ Munch** (dedicated inclusive meetup, monthly)
- **Newcomers' Munch** (specifically for first-timers, monthly)

### 3.2 Newcomer Buddy System

**What**: Pair every new member with an experienced community member for their first 2 events

**How to implement**:
1. Build a roster of volunteer buddies (experienced, vetted, friendly members)
2. When new member is approved, match them with a buddy based on interests and availability
3. Buddy introduces newcomer at their first event, answers questions, checks in throughout evening
4. Buddy follows up after event via message
5. After 2 buddy-accompanied events, newcomer can attend independently
6. Collect feedback from both newcomer and buddy

**Cost**: Gs. 0 (volunteer-based)
**Incentive for buddies**: Free event entry when buddying, recognition, token bonus
**Timeline**: Launch with first event
**KPIs**: Buddy assignments per month, newcomer satisfaction, newcomer retention rate

### 3.3 Peer Support Groups

**What**: Regular facilitated small-group check-ins for community emotional support

**Groups to offer**:

| Group | For | Facilitator | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Newcomer circle** | People in first 6 months of kink exploration | Experienced community member | Bi-weekly |
| **Relationship navigation** | People in non-traditional relationships (poly, open, D/s) | Trained facilitator | Bi-weekly |
| **Coming out circle** | People exploring/disclosing kink identity | Trained facilitator | Monthly |
| **Aftercare circle** | Post-event emotional processing | DM or trained facilitator | After each major event |
| **Identity exploration** | Gender, sexuality, role identity | Therapist + facilitator | Monthly |
| **Parents in kink** | Balancing family and lifestyle | Peer-led | Monthly |
| **Men's circle** | Masculinity, vulnerability, sexuality | Male facilitator | Monthly |

**How to implement**:
1. Recruit and train peer support facilitators (8-hour training program)
2. Set ground rules: confidentiality, no advice-giving (just listening), no judgment
3. Start with 1-2 groups, add more based on demand
4. Maximum 12 participants per group (intimate, safe)
5. Professional therapist on call for situations requiring clinical support

**Cost**: Gs. 200,000-500,000/month (venue, facilitator stipend for some groups)
**Revenue**: Gs. 0-50,000/session (some free, some nominal fee)
**Timeline**: Month 2-3

### 3.4 Community Mentorship Program

**What**: Formal 6-month pairing of experienced practitioners with eager learners

**Structure**:
1. **Application**: Both mentors and mentees apply with goals and interests
2. **Matching**: Staff matches pairs based on compatibility and learning goals
3. **Kick-off**: Group meeting with all mentor pairs — set expectations
4. **Monthly check-ins**: Pairs meet at least monthly (at events, over coffee, online)
5. **Mid-point review**: Staff checks in with all pairs at 3-month mark
6. **Graduation**: Recognition at community event, feedback collected

**Areas of mentorship**: Shibari, D/s dynamics, event production, community leadership, personal exploration

**Cost**: Gs. 500,000 setup (program materials, coordination)
**Revenue**: Gs. 100,000-200,000 program fee (covers admin)
**Timeline**: Year 2
**KPIs**: Mentor-mentee pairs, completion rate, satisfaction scores

## 4. Creative & Cultural Programs

### 4.1 Erotic Art Exhibition

**What**: Bi-annual showcase of local erotic artists across all visual mediums

**How to implement**:
1. Issue a call for submissions 3 months before exhibition
2. Jury of 3-5 people selects works (community + professional artists)
3. Rent gallery space or use event venue
4. Opening night event with wine, music, artist talks
5. Exhibition runs for 1-2 weeks (or 1-2 days at event venue)
6. Works available for sale (artist keeps 70-80%, community takes 20-30%)

**Media**: Painting, photography, sculpture, digital art, body art, textile/fiber art
**Cost**: Gs. 3,000,000 (venue, printing, wine, promotion)
**Revenue**: Gs. 50,000 entry x 50-100 visitors + art sales commission
**Timeline**: First exhibition at Sexpo 2026, standalone bi-annually after

### 4.2 Community Podcast — "Sin Tabues"

**What**: Weekly 45-60 minute podcast on sexuality, relationships, kink, and culture in Paraguay

**Episode types**:
- **Interview**: Guest speakers — educators, community members, health professionals, artists
- **Discussion**: Two hosts discuss a topic with community call-in questions
- **Education**: Deep dive into a specific topic (consent, shibari, polyamory, etc.)
- **Story**: Community member shares their journey (anonymized if needed)
- **Current events**: Commentary on news related to sexuality, rights, culture

**How to implement**:
1. Invest in basic recording equipment (Gs. 2,000,000 — microphones, audio interface)
2. Record weekly (can batch 2-3 episodes per session)
3. Edit using free software (Audacity, GarageBand)
4. Distribute via Spotify for Podcasters (free), Apple Podcasts, YouTube
5. Promote each episode on social media with audiogram clips
6. Seek sponsorships once audience reaches 500+ per episode

**Cost**: Gs. 2,000,000 setup + Gs. 300,000/month (hosting, editing time)
**Revenue**: Sponsorships (Year 2+), audience building drives all other revenue
**Timeline**: Month 3-4
**KPIs**: Downloads per episode, subscriber growth, audience retention

### 4.3 Community Zine — "Maskarada Stories"

**What**: Quarterly physical/digital publication with community stories, art, reviews, and interviews

**Content per issue**:
- 2-3 personal essays or stories from community members
- 1-2 educational articles
- Erotic fiction or poetry
- Event reviews and photography
- Product reviews (Fun4Me cross-promotion)

3. Design in Canva Pro or InDesign
4. Print short run (50-100 copies) at local print shop + distribute PDF
5. Sell physical copies at events, distribute digital to members

**Cost**: Gs. 1,500,000/issue (printing + design)
**Revenue**: Gs. 30,000/physical copy x 50 + advertising income
**Timeline**: First issue 6 months in
**KPIs**: Copies sold/distributed, contributor submissions, advertising revenue

## 5. Digital Programs

### 5.1 Telegram Community Management

**What**: Private Telegram group as primary online community platform

**Channels to create**:
- **General chat**: Community discussion (moderated)
- **Events & announcements**: One-way channel for updates
- **Education**: Share articles, resources, discussion of educational topics
- **Classifieds**: Community buy/sell/trade
- **Recommendations**: Venue, restaurant, professional recommendations
- **Support**: Peer support and check-in channel
- **Fun**: Memes, jokes, off-topic

**Moderation rules**:
1. No personal attacks, harassment, or bullying
2. No sharing of explicit content in general channels (designated channel if needed)
3. No outing of community members
4. No solicitation of paid services without admin approval
5. Respect pronouns and identity
6. Moderators can remove content and issue warnings

**How to implement**:
1. Create group, invite initial community members
2. Establish and pin rules
3. Assign 2-3 volunteer moderators
4. Build Telegram bot for automated commands (/events, /tokens, /apply, etc.)
5. Post regular discussion prompts to keep engagement active

**Cost**: Gs. 0 (Telegram is free)
**Timeline**: Week 1

### 5.2 Newsletter — "Hilos" (Threads)

**What**: Monthly email newsletter with community updates, education, and exclusive offers

**Content per issue**:
- Community news and upcoming events
- 1-2 educational articles
- Fun4Me product spotlight (with discount code)
- Event recap with approved photos
- Community member spotlight (with consent)
- Podcast episode highlights
- Fun4Me sale/promotion

**How to implement**:
1. Choose platform (Brevo/Mailchimp, free tier)
2. Design template matching brand identity
3. Build email list: website signup, event registration, membership
4. Send monthly (first week of month)
5. Track opens, clicks, and conversions

**Cost**: Gs. 0-100,000/month (free tier platforms)
**Revenue**: Indirect (drives event attendance, product sales)
**Timeline**: Month 2
**KPIs**: Subscriber count, open rate (target >30%), click rate (target >5%)

### 5.3 Online Workshop Platform

**What**: Live online workshops via Zoom/Google Meet for remote participants

**How to implement**:
1. Identify workshops suitable for online delivery (education, discussion, demonstration)
2. Set up paid registration through Fun4Me website or Eventbrite
3. Use Zoom/Google Meet (free for up to 100 participants on Meet)
4. Record sessions with participant consent
5. Add recordings to member video library (on-demand access)

**Workshops for online delivery**:
- BDSM 101
- Consent & negotiation
- Couples communication
- Relationship discussions
- Product education (Fun4Me demos)
- Sexual health topics
- Kink theory and history

**Not suitable for online**:
- Shibari (needs physical presence)
- Impact play (safety requires in-person)
- Play parties (obviously)

**Cost**: Gs. 0-500,000/month (platform costs)
**Revenue**: Gs. 50,000-150,000/ticket, unlimited geographic reach
**Timeline**: Month 3-4
